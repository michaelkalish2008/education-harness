# Plan

Plans are built backward from the learner's question, never from a syllabus. Keep it small and quick. Principles: 0, 2, 3, 7, 8.

1. **Honor the lesson mode.** Read the accepted profile choice. For `structured`, create the complete three-to-five-step plan before teaching. For `semi-structured`, plan only the next coherent segment (one or two steps), then revise from the learner's direction without discarding worked steps. For `free-form`, do not impose a project plan: follow the current question and create a plan only if the learner asks for one or their question naturally becomes a project.
2. **Honor sociability.** `group` plans include roles or a shared task; `guided` plans identify what the tutor, teacher or parent does without doing the learner's work; `solo` plans keep every activity independently workable. Never assume other people are available merely because a share-out exists.
3. **Honor mastery modality.** For `written-practical`, `strong_version_shows` names a written explanation plus observable work (calculation, code, query, artifact or parity report). For `spoken`, it names an oral teach-back and responsive explanation; record the learner's words as their attempt, but do not force a written artifact. For `both`, use complementary written/practical work and spoken explanation before calling the learner's own work drafted. A formal defense remains optional in every mode.
4. **Honor depth as a destination.** Use `program.depth_modes`. Civic overview uses concrete decisions and critical questions with no calculus or code owed. Foundational uses small mathematical examples with optional code. Practitioner completes all four stages. Advanced adds derivations, assumptions, failure modes and extensions. Reduce module count as well as within-module detail. Never treat civic understanding as failed professional training.
5. **Honor learning context without narrowing education to a credential.** Interview plans include explanation and implementation practice relevant to the target role. School plans fit vocabulary, prerequisites and evidence to the stated level without teaching to a score. Curiosity/personal-growth plans follow the learner's questions. Professional-growth plans connect to the stated role and responsibilities.
6. **Calibrate before content.** The learner has stated what they want to do, and the tutor has checked the smallest relevant background/notation assumptions.
7. **Check prerequisite references.** Inspect `where_am_i`'s `standards.matches`; cite only genuinely relevant statements. Standards describe a step but never set scope.
8. **The question** comes from their life or words. For the data-science path, use the idea's `example_hint`.
   - If the learner supplies a public URL, call `library(action="add_url", url=..., area=...)` before planning. Use only text the page actually exposes. If it is inaccessible, too large, unsupported or gated, say what was unavailable and ask for another public URL or a local/exported copy. Summarize the captured source into the library, distinguish the source's sequence from your adaptation, and pass `sources=[{kind: "url", url, title, library_id, accessed_on}]` to `write_plan` so the plan preserves provenance. A URL informs the plan; it does not override the learner's goal, profile, standards check or prerequisite calibration.
   - A URL may be a news article or other current-event source. Use its visible publication date, publisher and byline when available; retain the access date. Separate what the article reports, what a quoted person claims, the author's analysis and the economic model used to interpret it. For a lesson such as the economics of energy, connect the event to durable ideas—supply and demand, elasticity, externalities, market structure, regulation, investment and distributional effects—at the learner's chosen depth. Clearly mark time-sensitive numbers and contested claims, and corroborate them with current primary or authoritative sources when they materially affect the lesson. Do not treat one article as a complete or neutral account.
   - If the learner supplies a screenshot, scan, diagram or phone photo, inspect only what is visibly present. If the host exposes a local file path, register it with `library(action="add", path=..., area=...)`, write a concise summary of the visible educational content, and pass `sources=[{kind: "image", title, library_id}]` to `write_plan`. If there is no durable path, the image may still inform the current plan; identify it as an attached image in `sources` and tell the learner it will need to be attached again in a later chat. Ask about illegible, cropped or ambiguous material instead of inventing it. Treat faces, names, grades and other personal details as private and omit them unless they are essential and the learner asks to use them.
   - Photos may be experiential anchors, not just documents to transcribe. If a learner shares a photo of an apple falling and asks to learn gravity, begin from the observable event and their question, then distinguish observation (the apple changes position) from interpretation and model (gravity, acceleration, air resistance). Build the lesson toward the learner's chosen depth and mastery mode; do not claim a still image establishes motion, timing, force or cause that it cannot show by itself.
   - Treat an attached video the same way: use only frames and audio the host can actually inspect, identify it with `kind: "video"`, and register a local path with `library(action="add", ...)` when available. A video can support observations about change over time, but measurements such as speed or acceleration require a visible time scale and distance reference; otherwise keep them qualitative. Ask for a clip, transcript or description if the host cannot inspect the media. Never infer unseen events.
   - The default data-science path uses `anchor_project`, a recommendation system. Begin with the current idea's concrete recommendation-system milestone so the learner knows why the work matters. Then teach the idea's `foundation` independently, without reducing it to an application trick. Return to build or evaluate the milestone, and end by making `converges_on_transformers` concrete: name whether the idea is inside the transformer or supports it externally, where it appears, and what it does there.
   - Each idea follows `learning_stages`: math with no code required; from-scratch code; an appropriate package implementation; then a coding-harness parity check. Use the idea's `practice` details. For stochastic training, parity means fixed inputs and seeds plus objective, gradient, invariant or one-step agreement—not identical full training trajectories.
9. **A tiny example** is workable by hand and comes from their interests when known.
10. **Plan size:** structured uses three to five steps; semi-structured uses one or two. Each step is `{id, does, concepts, strong_version_shows, requires, terms?, standards_cited?}`.
    - `terms`: each word the step uses that has an everyday meaning, with the one meaning it has here as a concrete phrase, e.g. `{"average": "the average score over all views, with a click scored 1 and a non-click 0 (1 click in 10 views is 0.1)"}`. Pick one word per idea and don't reuse a word for a second thing.
11. **Explorers:** for a step whose idea turns on a quantity the learner can vary, name the explorer in `strong_version_shows` (what to vary, what to watch), so the tutor builds it at the start of the step.
12. **Share-out:** the final structured step is a share-out. Semi-structured adds one only when useful.
13. `write_plan(project, question, steps, example, audience, sources?)` when the chosen mode calls for a plan.
14. **Solutions:** skip by default; work them out and check them on request.
15. Tell the learner the plan or current segment in two or three lines, then start below the first unconfirmed prerequisite.

For a goal with several parts, propose the path in plain words after the first session, from what they actually did, and save it with `set_arc` on their yes. Choose the lens that fits (math, philosophy, general) and say it in one line.
